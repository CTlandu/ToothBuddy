Case 01 · Mindfulness

Headspace

Hand-drawn editorial illustration. The "big warm hug" school.

App Store · Headspace: Meditation & Sleep

We were getting feedback that the brand felt childish and cartoony, so we rebranded — more vibrant and simplified, more approachable to all age groups. — Karen Hong, Brand Illustrator at Headspace
I like to think of our brand as a big warm hug. We try to make our work fun and witty while still being considerate to sensitive, heavier topics. — Karen Hong

Design vocabulary

  • Hand-drawn feel — Procreate-style brushwork, intentionally "imperfect" lines, no vector crispness.
  • Saturated but warm — coral, mustard, deep teal; rarely pastel, never neon.
  • Characters as anchors, not as decoration — a turtle for "Patience," birds for "Interrupted Sleep." Each illustration is a single visual metaphor.
  • Editorial scale — illustrations occupy half the screen on key moments; UI shrinks around them.
  • Restraint on micro-interactions — fewer particle bursts, more quiet animation.
Palette
TypeApricot, sans-serif
If we mimic this for ToothBuddy: commission (or hand-illustrate) ~6 small character vignettes — a tooth napping at night, a tooth jogging in the morning, the family group as a tooth-family. UI recedes; illustrations carry the brand. Adult-leaning but kids enjoy the storytelling.
Case 02 · Habit + Education

Duolingo

Geometric character system. Kids love it; adults pay for it.

App Store · Duolingo: Language Lessons · Brand book · design.duolingo.com

Our characters are diverse, quirky, and lovable. But remember that our characters need to scale in terms of both size and production. When the pieces are simple, a character is much easier to pose in other positions. — Duolingo Brand Guidelines (Characters)
We use five main eye styles. Different styles can be explored, but make sure they're geometric in nature. No ovals. — Duolingo Brand Guidelines

Design vocabulary (the actual rules)

  • 1–2 basic shapes per body part (head, body, hair).
  • 5 eye styles: round, glasses, almond, linear, dots. No ovals. Ever.
  • Asymmetric mouth for emotion. Mouth can break the face outline for strong feelings.
  • Saturated flat fills + a single feather-green primary (their brand color).
  • Same character in many poses — repetition + pose variation builds a cast.
  • Custom letterforms in the logotype (Feather Bold) — rounded, friendly, child-readable.
Palette
TypeFeather Bold / Nunito-like
If we mimic this for ToothBuddy: design a small cast of tooth-characters following the same rules — 1–2 shapes, 5 eye styles, asymmetric mouths. Saturated flat fills. Plaque is a tiny goblin. Brush head is a friend. Adult-respectable because the geometry is disciplined, not random doodle. Kids love it because it's a cast they can come back to.
Case 03 · Self-Care Pet · Age 9+

Finch

Pastel pet companion. 701K ratings · 4.9★ · Apple Editors' Choice.

App Store · Finch: Self-Care Pet

Right off the bat, the game is very cute. It has a colorful, cartoony art style that creates a very welcoming environment. — Sophia Serice, app reviewer (Viking News)
A feature very interesting for a mental health app is the ability to connect with friends — strength, hugs, high fives, dances to help out your friends with their day. — Same review

Design vocabulary

  • One single companion character with deep customization (the bird grows, dresses up, evolves) — the whole UI is about caring for it.
  • Pastel-on-pastel palette — soft pink, peach, lavender, mint; almost no dark UI chrome.
  • Cards / rooms metaphor — your bird lives in a literal room you decorate. The "screen" is a scene, not a list.
  • Affirmation language — "send a hug," "high five," not "tap to like." Reframes every interaction.
  • Soft 2D illustration — like children's-book pages, but the writing is for adults dealing with anxiety, ADHD, grief.
Palette
TypePoppins-like rounded
If we mimic this for ToothBuddy: the app stops being a "tracker" and becomes a small room with a single tooth-character that grows. Streaks become the character's evolution. Family is the room visit list. Heavy commit — this changes the information architecture, not just the visuals. But the 701K-rating-with-mental-health-adults result is the strongest evidence that "very cartoony" can read as "very mature" when the framing is right.

How to pick

Reply with a number (01 / 02 / 03), or a blend (e.g., "02 character system + 01 hand-drawn feel", or "03 room-as-screen IA + 02 disciplined geometry"). I'll then:

  1. Build one high-fidelity ToothBuddy "Brush home" mockup in that lane (not four).
  2. Sketch the small cast of characters / illustration vocabulary the choice implies, so we're not just changing colors.
  3. Once you sign off, port the tokens to Theme.swift and migrate one screen end-to-end in the real app — you compare in the simulator, not in HTML.

What I'm explicitly not doing this time: inventing a "Mint Studio" or "Aurora Glass" from thin air. Every direction here points at a shipped, profitable app whose design has been stress-tested by millions of users — including kids and the adults they live with.
